Albert Einstein

1879 – 1955 | Theoretical Physicist & Visionary

About Albert Einstein

Albert Einstein was a German-born theoretical physicist who is widely regarded as one of the greatest scientists in history. He developed the theory of relativity, which fundamentally transformed our understanding of space, time, and gravity. In 1921, he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his explanation of the photoelectric effect. His famous equation, E = mc², established the relationship between mass and energy and laid the foundation for modern physics.

Important Events

  1. 1879: Born in Ulm, Kingdom of Württemberg, German Empire.
  2. 1905: Published four revolutionary physics papers during his "Annus Mirabilis".
  3. 1915: Formulated the General Theory of Relativity.
  4. 1921: Received the Nobel Prize in Physics.
  5. 1933: Moved to the United States and joined the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.
  6. 1955: Passed away in Princeton, New Jersey at the age of 76.

Key Inventions & Contributions

  • Theory of Special & General Relativity
  • Explanation of the Photoelectric Effect
  • Theory of Brownian Motion
  • Mass-Energy Equivalence Formula (E = mc²)
